# 0078. The scene/rendering benchmark tasks 02 to 10: what each tests, how the expected pixels are derived independently of both references, how the lit tasks are calibrated and what tolerance they need, and what the two sides could not both express

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Blueprint origin: M6 (blueprint §11), task M6-08 (the scene/rendering part); blueprint §12.2; `spec/ai-and-benchmarks.md` §6 (task format, set composition, baseline fairness); decisions 0021 (task format, one definition of success for both sides), 0074 (`mtek test`), 0077 (the harness `tools/ai-eval` that validates a project layer by layer), 0080 and 0081 (the lighting model and the camera exposure the lit tasks depend on).
- Supersedes: nothing. Extends the five seed tasks of decision 0021 to ten scene/rendering tasks (`scene-rendering-01` to `-10`).

## Context

`spec/ai-and-benchmarks.md` §6.2 asks for 10 scene/rendering tasks; the repository had one. A task is only a measurement if (a) its prompt says the same, precisely, to a model writing Mtek and to a model writing three.js, (b) the fixtures that define success were not obtained by running either solution, (c) both reference solutions pass them on hardware and (d) the starters fail them. The fixtures are shared (`mtek-tests/*.test.toml`, executed by `mtek test` and by the Playwright interpreter of the baseline support module, decision 0021 item 8), so a fixture is limited to what both sides can show: pixels of a 128 x 128 `rgba8unorm-srgb` target. Every choice below is a **proposal** unless a source is named.

## Decision

1. **The nine tasks** (all category `scene-rendering`, budgets 3 repairs, 4000 output tokens, 300 s, like `-01`):

   | Task | Mode | What it tests (beyond a camera, a clear colour and flat colours) |
   |---|---|---|
   | `-02` tilted bar | cold | a rotation about Z (the angle is given by its cosine 0.6 and sine 0.8 so the end of the bar is the plain point (1.2, 1.6, 0)), a non-cubic box, a sphere that pokes through the front of the box (depth test), a custom field of view and far plane |
   | `-03` arm | cold | a three-level hierarchy (`Arm` > `Elbow` > `Fore`, `Hand`) with parent-relative position, rotation and a uniform parent scale (0.75): a child must move, turn and scale with its parent; nodes with no shape; nested names (`Demo.Arm.Elbow.Hand`) |
   | `-04` off-axis | cold | a look-at camera that is not on an axis (5, 4, 9 to 0, 1, 0), a one-sided floor plane that faces up, occlusion between three solids (declared in the opposite order of their depth) |
   | `-05` orthographic | edit | switch a perspective camera to an orthographic one (height 8, 45 degrees down), move an entity, add a sphere half sunk into the floor; the starter shows the old scene |
   | `-06` directional light | cold | a physically based non-metal under one directional light given by its travel direction plus an ambient term; three faces of a box with the cosines 0.8, 0.6 and 0 (the last lit only by the ambient term), a floor |
   | `-07` point light | cold | one point light with inverse-square falloff and no range on a sphere and a floor (the brightness varies over the floor), ambient 5 % |
   | `-08` columns | edit | the leftmost of five columns exists; add four more of different heights and colours (a repeated, parametrised object: a prefab in Mtek, a function in three.js, a model may also just repeat it); the prompt names the geometry, not the abstraction |
   | `-09` clipping | cold | near and far planes cut boxes (a box whose front face is cut away so it is not filled in, a box cut at the far plane, a box beyond the far plane, a box behind the camera) and a hidden entity |
   | `-10` coloured lights | cold | two point lights of different colours (a red and a blue one add up to magenta where they overlap) and a tinted ambient light on a white ball and floor |

   Skills not repeated across the nine: rotation, hierarchy and scale, camera placement, projection kind, depth ordering, the plane mesh, the three light kinds (directional, point, ambient), colour arithmetic of lights, clipping, visibility, repetition. Seven tasks are cold (the Mtek starter is the one-line stub of `-01`, the baseline starter the empty skeleton of `-01`), two are edit tasks (`-05`, `-08`), whose starters are complete, compilable, type-checking programs that show the old scene.

2. **Expected pixels are derived from the scene the prompt describes, not from a reference.** `evidence/M6/benchmark-scene-rendering/scenes.mjs` transcribes each prompt into scene data; `oracle.mjs` casts one ray through every pixel centre (f64, no three.js, no Mtek runtime) with the conventions of `spec/scenes.md` and `spec/stdlib.md` §4: parent-composed `T·R·S`, look-at camera with up +Y, perspective and orthographic projections, entry-point hits on convex solids (a ray sees where it enters a box or sphere; back faces are culled), the near and far planes applied to the depth along the view axis, a one-sided plane, `visible = false` skipped. Lit surfaces are shaded with `shadePbr` of `tests/browser/support/reference-shading.ts`, the f64 oracle of `spec/materials.md` §8 that M4 compared with the GPU, at the camera's default exposure (scale π). `derive.mjs` then picks the pixels, deterministically: the projected centre of every entity (which, for a hidden, cut off or occluded entity, is a check that it is **not** there), and per surface (an entity, or one face of a box) and for the clear colour a few pixels whose rays up to 3 pixels around them hit the same surface ("inside", spread out by farthest-point sampling) and a few 1.5 to 2.25 pixels from its boundary (inside and outside), which pins silhouettes to about a pixel and never depends on a rasterisation rule or on how a sphere is tessellated (a ray through the band between the inscribed and the true radius makes a pixel ambiguous and it is dropped). The fixtures are the output of that tool, with the scene summary and the method in their header comments. `selfcheck.mjs` checks the oracle against the hand-derived, hardware-verified fixture of `scene-rendering-01` (21 of 21 pixels), against two analytic projections (`Tip`, `Hand`) and against the calibration of the lit tasks.

3. **Fairness.** (a) Every prompt states the scene in terms both APIs share: coordinates, sizes, colours as sRGB hex, radians, "visible", "near plane"; it names no API (no `Unlit`, `Pbr`, `quat`, `MeshStandardMaterial`, "prefab", "scale" for the columns). (b) Where the two stacks use different units, the prompt states the **observable** result and each side derives its own number: a light is "calibrated so that a perfectly matte white surface facing it squarely (at 3 units, for a point light) renders as exactly white"; the ambient light "adds k of the surface's own diffuse colour". In Mtek's units that is a directional light of 1 lux, a point light of `d²` candela at distance `d` (9 for `d = 3`), an ambient intensity of `k / π` (the default exposure scale π multiplies the ambient term as well, `spec/materials.md` §8 rule 9); in three.js it is intensity π, `9π` and `kπ` (a matte surface gets `I / π` of the colour). The Mtek references say so in comments, as the three.js references do. (c) The baseline references use the helpers of the support module where they exist (`boxMesh`, `sphereMesh`, `unlit`, `srgb`, `lookAtCamera`, `orthographicCamera`) and plain three.js where they do not (the plane mesh, the physically based material, the lights): see item 8. (d) The two references were written separately from the prompt; neither was consulted to derive a fixture. (e) The task descriptions of the lit tasks state the tolerance and the model the expectations come from, so a solver is not asked to guess either.

4. **Findings while verifying that changed a task.** (a) The first draft of the three lit tasks set the Mtek ambient intensity to the number in the prompt (0.1) instead of `0.1 / π`: the oracle, built from the same wrong transcription, agreed with the Mtek reference, and only the three.js reference (which got `0.1π`) disagreed, by up to 19 bytes. The cause was the exposure scale π on the ambient term, not a defect of either renderer; the prompts' observable wording was right and the oracle scene and the Mtek reference were wrong. Both were corrected (`0.1 / π`, `0.05 / π`), the fixtures regenerated, and `selfcheck.mjs` now pins the calibration (a white surface under ambient `0.1 / π` reads 89). It shows why the calibration is stated observably. (b) A box whose depth range straddles the near plane is not "cut open and filled": the clipped front face is gone and the back faces are culled, so the box looks hollow; the `-09` prompt says so and the oracle encodes it.

5. **Lit tasks: model differences and the tolerance.** `-06`, `-07` and `-10` use non-metals only (a metal receives no diffuse ambient in `spec/materials.md` §8 but gets a multi-scattering term from three.js's ambient light: the two stacks genuinely differ there, so the tasks avoid it) and rough surfaces. Measured on the hardware adapter (`mtek-lit-deviation.txt`, `measure-baseline-deviation.spec.ts`), the largest deviation of any fixture pixel from the oracle, per channel in bytes: Mtek reference 1, 4 and 1 (`-06`, `-07`, `-10`); three.js reference 2, 5 and 1. The two largest are the same dark pixel on the red ball (49, 47), which both renderers shade 4 to 5 bytes below the oracle (probably a tessellated sphere's interpolated normals against the exact sphere, in a very dark channel where the sRGB curve is steep; not investigated further, since both sides agree with each other). The tolerance of the lit pixels is **6** (the clear-colour pixels stay at 2), stated in the prompts; it is small against what the cases are about (a wrong light unit changes the linear brightness by a factor π; a missing second light or a missing ambient term changes the pixels it lights by far more than 6 bytes). (This is an argument, not a measurement.)

6. **What could not be both expressed and measured, and how the set was adjusted.** The set was designed within the Mtek v0.1 subset reported by `mtek context` (`supported` / `notSupported`), and every one of the nine tasks is solvable in it, so no task was weakened for lack of a language feature. Ideas dropped because the **common** harness cannot show them: textured surfaces (the baseline build, `support/test/build.ts`, has no loader for an image and `startTask` builds synchronously, so a model could not load the same PNG on both sides), instancing and draw-call counts (`expect_counter` is Mtek-only, and instancing is invisible in pixels), exact entity state (`expect_state` is Mtek-only). Visibility, clipping and culling are therefore tested through what they show (`-09`). Observations about the language that the tasks exercise and that a model must discover rather than be told: a prefab's `mesh` is a construction-only constant so a parametrised column height goes through `scale` (`-08`); a directional light's direction is the entity's rotation applied to -Z, so `(0, -0.6, -0.8)` is a rotation of `-asin(0.6)` about X (`-06`); nested `entity` blocks are parenting (`-03`).

7. **Verification on the Mtek side** (`evidence/M6/benchmark-scene-rendering/mtek/`, the JSON of `node tools/ai-eval/src/cli.ts validate`, decision 0077): every reference passes `parse`, `check`, `build`, `start` and `tests` and every required symbol, on the hardware adapter of the environment record (AMD Radeon RX 7900 XTX, `isFallbackAdapter` false), 336 pixel expectations in 9 fixtures in all. The seven cold starters fail `check` with `MTEK-E9006` (the entry scene `Demo` is not declared), so `build`, `start` and `tests` are NOT-RUN with kind `skipped`; the two edit starters (`-05`, `-08`) compile and fail the first pixel that differs (the crate in the wrong place, the second column missing). `mtek_side_status` of the nine tasks is `"verified"` on that evidence; `starter_diagnostics` is not set (it is for starters that must produce a particular diagnostic).

8. **Verification on the baseline side** (`baseline-reference-results.json`, `baseline-starter-results.json`, from `MTEK_REQUIRE_GPU=1 npm run test:benchmarks` filtered to the nine tasks, sanitised with `npm run evidence:sanitize`): the nine three.js references pass (9 of 9), the nine starters fail (0 of 9) at the first pixel, the cold ones as the empty black scene of the skeleton. **Requests for shared files** (not changed here): `benchmarks/baselines/support/test/starters.test.ts` lists tasks by name, so the starters and references of `-02` to `-10` are type-checked only by `tsc -b` (`npm run check:ts`), not by that test; the support module has no helper for the plane mesh, the physically based material or the lights, so the lit and plane baselines use raw three.js (a `planeMesh`, `pbr` and light helpers would also make the baseline side fairer for future tasks and would be counted in the baseline's documentation cost); `benchmarks/README.md` still describes five tasks.

## Fixtures

`benchmarks/tasks/scene-rendering-NN/mtek-tests/scene.test.toml` for `NN` = 02 to 10: one step, then `expect_pixel` steps only (25, 38, 58, 39, 39, 24, 45, 44 and 24 pixels), generated by `evidence/M6/benchmark-scene-rendering/derive.mjs` (`node derive.mjs scene-rendering-NN --write FILE`; `--map` prints the surfaces the oracle sees). Tolerance 2 (unlit and the clear colour of every task) or 6 (lit surfaces of `-06`, `-07`, `-10`).

## Consequences

- The scene/rendering category has ten tasks, each with a starter, fixtures and a verified reference on both sides. `benchmarks/README.md` (status table, seed-task table) is stale until the orchestrator updates it.
- The three lit tasks make the exposure convention (π) part of what is measured. That is a property of the models' knowledge of their framework's units, not a trick: the prompt says what must be seen.
- Fixtures are reproducible: a task's prompt, `scenes.mjs` and `derive.mjs` determine its fixture byte for byte; changing a prompt means changing `scenes.mjs` and regenerating (and then re-verifying both references).
- No language, compiler or runtime behaviour changed.
