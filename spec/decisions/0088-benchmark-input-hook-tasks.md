# 0088. The benchmark tasks that use the input and tick hooks: `interaction-09` (key release), `interaction-10` (held keys), `interaction-11` (the fixed tick); derivations, fairness, mutation checks, two baseline fixes and a three.js finding

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Blueprint origin: M6 (blueprint §11), the benchmark (blueprint §12.2); `spec/scenes.md` §§7, 10, 10.2, 10.3 (events, `is_key_down`, frame order, time policy); `spec/ai-and-benchmarks.md` §6 (task format, set composition, baseline fairness); decisions 0021 (task format, fixtures), 0074 (`mtek test`), 0077 (the `validate` command), 0079 (the interaction tasks 02 to 08: geometry, oracle, timing rules, mutation method), 0086 (the baseline hooks `onKeyUp`, `isKeyDown`, `fixedUpdate` and the frame order).
- Supersedes: nothing. Corrects two details of the baseline frame clock that decision 0086 item 4 introduced (items 6 and 7 below); the rest of 0086 stands.

## Context

Decision 0079 item 7 and decision 0086 recorded that no task of the benchmark used a key release, a held key (`is_key_down`) or `fixed_update`, because the baseline support could not express them; 0086 added the hooks and said no task was authored, because the set was fixed at 30 + 8 tasks (blueprint §12.2). The set owner has now asked for three more tasks of the category interaction, so **the task set grows from 30 to 33 tasks** (the interaction category from 8 to 11; the holdout stays at 8). `spec/ai-and-benchmarks.md` §6.2 and `benchmarks/README.md` still say 30 and 8 (item 9). The new tasks must be solvable in Mtek (what `mtek context` says this build supports) and in three.js WebGPU with the support hooks, with the same `.test.toml` fixtures defining success for both sides, prompts free of either side's API names, and every expected value derived from the task statement by an independent model.

Every choice below is a **proposal** (a design choice) unless it states a measurement.

## Decision

1. **The three tasks** (all `edit` tasks, budgets `max_repairs = 3`, `max_output_tokens = 4000`, `max_wall_seconds = 300`; the geometry is that of decision 0079 item 2: an orthographic camera at (0, 0, 10), `height = 12.8`, 10 pixels per world unit, flat colours on `#202020`).

   | Task | What the model does | What it tests | Fixtures / pixel checks | Required symbols |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | `interaction-09` | a spring-loaded launcher: the Stone (2 x 2, yellow) is orange while Space is held; while Space is held each Up press adds 1 to `power` (at most 5, shown by the block `Power`); the **release** of Space launches the Stone 2 units per point of power (centre never beyond x = 5) and resets `power`; a press of Space or a release of Up does nothing; key events count in the order they happened, also within one frame | `key_up`; state tracked from press and release; a release that acts and a press that does not; a cap; same-frame order (`Space down, Up down, Space up` launches with power 1; `Up down, Space down` does not count the Up; two Up presses in one frame count twice) | 4 / 425 | `Demo.Stone`, `Demo.Power`, `Demo.power` |
   | `interaction-10` | the arrow keys fly a Ship at 8 units per second per held key along each axis, in every frame in which the key is held; keys held together act together, opposite keys cancel; a key is held from the frame that handles its press up to but not including the frame that handles its release; a press and a release within one frame is held in no frame; the centre is limited to [-4, 4] on each axis after every frame | held keys (`is_key_down`) polled in `update`; per-frame motion proportional to the frame duration; two or four keys at once; the press frame counts and the release frame does not; clamping; a tap that moves nothing | 5 / 390 | `Demo.Ship`, `Demo.Field` |

   Every task has one **retention fixture** that the starter passes (nothing moves without input; for `interaction-11` four frames of 0.001 s, less than a step, so no tick and a Glider shift of a quarter of a pixel that no pixel shows). The starter of each task draws the whole scene and does nothing else; `interaction-09` also declares `power` and draws `Power` from it, as `interaction-06` does with `value`. `interaction-11` is in the category interaction because the set owner asked for it there; it uses no key (time is its input).

2. **The fixtures are derived, not recorded.** `evidence/M6/benchmark-input-hooks/oracle/` has `lib.mjs` (on top of the painter and writer of decision 0079: probes just inside and just outside every edge, and `FrameTime`, the frame clock of §10 and §10.2 as arithmetic) and one model per task (`interaction-NN.mjs`): the prompt as a few lines of JavaScript (a queue of key events and a state machine; the key state at the end of the input phase, then the movement; the accumulator), no engine and no reference. `node oracle/interaction-NN.mjs` rewrites `mtek-tests/*.test.toml`; every expected colour was computed there; the header of each fixture says so and states the derivation (geometry, speeds, frame lengths). The oracle refuses a box with an edge off the pixel grid, a key press of a held key or a release of a key that is not held (the runtime ignores both), and (below) a frame whose tick count is not robust.
   - `interaction-10` frames last 1/16 s (0.5 unit = 5 pixels, exact in binary), 1/32 s and 3/32 s (also exact, below the 0.1 s clamp); positions are multiples of 0.1 at every checkpoint, which the oracle enforces.
   - `interaction-11`: the Glider's edge is at pixel 4 + 60 x (program time), which falls between pixel boundaries, so the oracle only places a probe whose pixel centre is at least 0.2 pixel from the edge and refuses a checkpoint otherwise. The tick counts come from `FrameTime`: after 5, 7, 9, 12 and 20 frames of 0.024 s (1.44 steps) the ticks are 7, 10, 12, 17 and 28 (floor of 1.44 n); a frame of 0.0867 s (5.202 steps) after three frames of 1/60 s runs 4 ticks (not 5), drops 1 step and keeps 0.202; a frame of 0.0142 s (0.852 step) then runs 1 tick (0 if the fraction were dropped, 2 if the backlog were kept), the next 0, the next 1; a frame of 0.5 s counts as 0.1 s: 4 ticks and a Glider shift of 6 pixels (30 if the clamp were missing).

3. **Tick robustness: the step is not the same number on both sides, and exact multiples are fragile.** The Mtek manifest carries `runtime.fixed_step` as the binary32 print `0.016666668` (`crates/mtek-compiler/src/project/config.rs`, `DEFAULT_FIXED_STEP`); the baseline clock used `1 / 60` in binary64. They differ by 1.3e-9 s, so a frame that is an exact multiple of a step (1/60 s, 1/30 s, 0.1 s = 6 steps, or the sum of such frames) can give a different tick count, or a different carried fraction (0 against almost one step after a dropped surplus), on the two sides. Fixtures must not depend on it. `FrameTime` runs **both** values of the step and refuses any frame that gives a different tick count or dropped count, or that ends within 0.001 step of a whole number of steps; the frame lengths are therefore 1/60 s and 1/30 s **rounded up in the fourth digit** (0.0167 and 0.0334) and a few others (0.024, 0.0867, 0.0142, 0.001), and the 0.5 s frame is the last frame of its fixture (after a clamped frame the carried fraction is 0 or one step, depending on rounding noise, on every implementation, so no frame follows it). The clamp itself is the runtime's job and a solution cannot get it wrong; what the long frame checks is that the Glider follows the frame's counted duration and that no more than 4 ticks run.

4. **The prompts fix what the specification leaves open.** `spec/scenes.md` §10 says "backlog beyond the catch-up limit is discarded"; it does not say whether the fraction of a step that remains is part of the backlog. The runtime keeps it (`accumulator %= fixedStep`, `packages/runtime-web/src/schedule/scheduler.ts`), and so does the baseline clock; the prompt of `interaction-11` says "whole steps beyond the fourth are dropped … what is less than one step is kept", and a fixture separates the three readings (keep the fraction, drop all, keep the backlog). **A gap of the specification, recorded here, not changed.** The prompt of `interaction-10` says which frames count as held (the frame that handles the press, up to but not including the frame that handles the release), which is the reading of §7.3 ("as of the end of phase 1 of the current frame") and of §10.3; `interaction-09` says that key events are handled in the order in which they happened, with an example, as `interaction-06` does.

5. **Fairness.** The prompts say what must happen in terms of the picture and the keys, never in terms of an API (no `key_up`, `is_key_down`, `fixed_update`, `update`, `bind`, `onKeyUp`, `isKeyDown`, `fixedUpdate`). Each baseline starter is the same scene built with the support helpers; the hooks the baseline needs are the ones decision 0086 gave `TaskHost` and `TaskApp`, with the same capabilities as Mtek's (`host.onKeyUp(code, handler)`, `host.isKeyDown(code)`, `app.fixedUpdate(dt)`); a baseline solution may also keep its own key set or its own accumulator (the alternates of item 8 do, and pass). What each side must do differently is part of the cost being measured: Mtek has `on key_up(Key.Space)`, `is_key_down(Key.ArrowLeft)` and `fixed_update`, the baseline registers handlers and keeps its own references.

6. **A defect of 0086's frame clock: the held-key state inside a handler.** `FrameClock` updated `down` one transition at a time and called each handler right after, so in the `key_down` handler of a key that went down and up again in the same frame, `isKeyDown` answered true; the runtime (`InputState.deliver`) applies the whole batch first and `is_key_down` answers "as of the end of phase 1" (§7.3). `FrameClock.frame` now applies every transition to `down` and then dispatches the handlers. Test `answers is_key_down as of the END of the input phase, also inside a handler of that phase` (`benchmarks/baselines/support/test/frame.test.ts`). No fixture of the earlier 38 tasks depended on it.

7. **A defect of 0086's frame clock: the step.** `FIXED_STEP` is now `0.016666668`, the value the Mtek manifest carries (item 3), instead of `1 / 60`. The test that said "3 frames of 1/40 s run 1, 2 and 1 ticks" was an exact multiple (1.5 steps three times, 4.5 steps) and gives 1, 1 and 2 with the manifest's step; it was replaced by frames of 0.024 s (1, 1, 2) and a test that a frame of exactly 0.016666668 s is one tick and one of twice that two. The 8 tests of `frame.test.ts` pass; no earlier task uses `fixedUpdate`.

8. **The checks that the fixtures are the right ones (mutation and alternates).** `evidence/M6/benchmark-input-hooks/mutation/run.mjs` validates a Mtek candidate through the harness's `validate` command (parse, check, build, start, fixtures on the real browser); `baseline-mutants.mjs` runs three.js candidates through the Playwright interpreter.
   - **Mtek: 29 mutants, all fail (29 of 29); 4 alternates, all pass (4 of 4).** Every mutant compiles, so it is the fixtures that reject it. `interaction-09` (10): launch on press, no limit at x = 5, Up counting without Space, power not reset, the release ignored for the state, a cap of 6, 1 unit per point, the release of Up also launching, `is_key_down(Space)` inside the Up handler instead of the tracked state, polling edges in `update` (key events not handled). `interaction-10` (10): one move per press, 0.5 per frame instead of the frame duration, no clamp, a limit of 5, else-if chains (opposite keys do not cancel), no diagonal, a sticky direction (release ignored), movement in `fixed_update`, the key state of the previous frame (the press frame skipped, the release frame counted), an extra step on a press. `interaction-11` (9): a tick per frame, whole steps of the frame's own duration (no carry), rounded steps, an own accumulator without the cap, with the whole accumulator emptied by a drop, and keeping the backlog, the Glider moved per tick, per frame, the Ticker from `frame.time`. The alternates: the Stone's x as bound state with a 0/1 colour state; held flags set by `key_down`/`key_up` handlers with bound state; an own accumulator in `update` (no `fixed_update`) that implements the stated rules; positions written directly in `fixed_update` and `update`.
   - **three.js: 19 mutants, all fail (19 of 19); 3 alternates, all pass (3 of 3).** `interaction-09` (6): launch on press, Up without Space, no limit at x = 5, power not reset, polling edges in `update`, `isKeyDown("Space")` in the Up handler; alternate: the colour set in `update` from the held state. `interaction-10` (6): one move per press, 0.5 per frame, movement in `fixedUpdate`, a sticky direction (release ignored), else-if chains, the key state of the previous frame; alternate: held flags kept from `onKeyDown`/`onKeyUp`. `interaction-11` (7): a tick per frame, whole steps of the frame's own duration, the Glider per tick, per frame, an own accumulator without the cap, keeping the backlog, emptying the whole accumulator; alternate: an own accumulator in `update` that implements the stated rules (no `fixedUpdate`). The mutants that fail only one fixture (no limit at x = 5, polling, `isKeyDown` in the handler, 0.5 per frame, else-if chains, the two accumulator variants) are each caught by the fixture built for them.
   Results: `mutation/mutants-results.json`, `alternates-results.json`, `baseline-results.json`.

9. **Mutants and checks that the pixels cannot show.** The probes sit at pixel centres, so a difference of less than half a pixel in an edge is invisible (a movement that lags by 0.3 pixel is not a failure): `interaction-10`'s mutant "moves in `fixed_update`" is caught because the first frame of 1/16 s is 3 ticks of 1/60 s (0.4 unit against 0.5, one pixel). The clamp of the frame duration to 0.1 s cannot be gotten wrong by a solution (the harness and the runtime clamp it before the program sees it), so `interaction-11` cannot mutate it; it checks that the Glider follows the counted duration.

10. **A three.js finding that shaped the fixtures of `interaction-09`.** With the baseline's manual render loop, three.js r186.1 (WebGPU renderer, render target) **did not upload a material colour change made in the frame directly after a frame in which the mesh's position changed**, and the colour then stays stale for good (the value is cached as uploaded). Reproduction found with the first draft of `power-is-capped-and-needs-space-held.test.toml`: press Space (the Stone turns orange), press Up, release Up, release Space (the Stone moves and turns yellow), then press Space in the **next** frame: the Stone stays yellow, although the handler ran (a marker moved by the same state changed) and although the colour was set again every frame in `update`. With one idle frame between the two, or without the move, the colour is uploaded. Setting the position with `set` instead of `.x`, calling `nodeFrame.update()` or `info.reset()` before each render (what the animation loop of three.js does) did not help. The cause was not diagnosed (a suspect is the partial upload of changed uniform ranges, `UniformsGroup.updateRanges`, in `renderers/common`); no workaround was added to the support module. The Mtek renderer is not affected (the same sequence passes). The oracle of `interaction-09` therefore **refuses a fixture that changes the Stone's colour in the frame after a frame that moved it**, and the fixtures keep an idle frame there. It is a risk for a model's three.js solution in a sequence the fixtures do not contain; reported to the orchestrator as a finding about the baseline, not a language gap.

11. **Language and harness gaps found.**
    - `Entity.material.param` for a built-in material is still not assignable (decisions 0079 and 0086): the colours of `interaction-09` are bound to a state through a function (`bind(stone_color(pulled))`), the idiomatic route in this build.
    - Nothing in the language needed a change for `key_up`, `is_key_down` or `fixed_update`; `mtek test`'s `press`, `release` and `step` express every fixture; `dt` of a `step` is clamped to 0.1 s like any frame delta (`Scheduler.stepManual`), and a fixture cannot give a frame that is longer to the program (the 0.5 s frame is clamped on both sides).
    - `spec/scenes.md` §10 does not say whether the fraction of a step survives a drop (item 4).
    - `expect_state` stays Mtek-only (decision 0021 item 8), so every fixture asserts pixels only; `power`, `ticks` and the held state are checked through what they draw.

12. **Status.** `mtek_side_status` is `verified` for the three tasks; their Mtek sources are formatted (`mtek fmt --check` is clean for every file of the three references and starters).

## Consequences

- The set has 33 tasks (10 scene-rendering, 11 interaction, 6 shader-bridge, 6 maintenance) and 8 holdout tasks. The validator counts tasks, it does not hard-code 30 or 8 (`npm run check:tasks` prints "33 tasks, 8 holdout tasks"). **For the orchestrator, not changed here:** `benchmarks/README.md` (the two "30 tasks"/"8 interaction" counts, the status table, and the "known gaps" row, which no longer applies to key-up, held-key and `fixed_update`), `spec/ai-and-benchmarks.md` §6.2 ("30 tasks: … 8 interaction"), and blueprint §12.2 if the set is to stay in line with it.
- `benchmarks/baselines/support/test/starters.test.ts` enumerates the task directories and covers the new tasks without a change.
- A model that implements its own accumulator on the baseline side meets the exact-multiple fragility of item 3 only if it uses frames that are exact multiples; the fixtures do not.
- The fixtures of `interaction-11` use frame lengths that are not exactly 1/60 s and 1/30 s (item 3); a recorded browser session would use neither.
- No model has been run on any task; their difficulty is unmeasured.
