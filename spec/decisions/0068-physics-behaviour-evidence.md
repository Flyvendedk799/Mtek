- Supersedes: nothing. Fulfils item 16 of decision 0066 (the engine-level checks of `spec/physics.md` §7 that were left out) and narrows one sentence of `spec/physics.md` §7 ("no CCD in v0.1") to what was measured (item 3).

## Context

`spec/physics.md` §7 and `spec/testing.md` §6.6 require browser evidence on the real engine for sensor enter/exit counts, the high-speed limitation, removal during collision callbacks, pause and resume, the same-frame combinations of `spec/scenes.md` §10.3, twenty mount/dispose cycles and a bounded plateau of live resources under 1 000 spawns and destroys in waves. Decision 0066 tested most of the *rules* against a recording fake backend and the resting contact, the impulse and twenty cycles (bodies and colliders only) against the engine. This record is the rest: every item below is a browser spec on the hardware adapter (manual clock, the manifest's fixed step, `MTEK_REQUIRE_GPU=1`) or an adapter test in Node, with every expected number **derived from the program** before it was compared with a run. The labels follow 0063: **measured** (a number from a run, asserted by a test), **derived** (computed by hand from the program and the documented model; the test compares it with the run), **hypothesis** (an explanation no test pins).

No runtime or adapter code changed: none of the specs revealed a defect in the M5-05 integration (item 9 lists the mutation checks that show the specs would have). Two observations are recorded as observations (items 7 and 8) rather than changed.

## Decision

1. **Shape.** Six new browser specs under `tests/browser/specs/m5/` (`physics-sensor`, `physics-tunnelling`, `physics-callbacks`, `physics-combinations`, `physics-pause`, `physics-resources`), five new fixtures under `tests/browser/fixtures/m5/` (`physics_sensor`, `physics_tunnel`, `physics_callbacks`, `physics_combos`, `physics_waves`; the existing `physics_drop` serves the pause spec), one Node test of the adapter (`packages/physics-rapier/src/tunnelling.test.ts`), the shared helpers of the specs (`specs/m5/physics-support.ts`: the free-fall and crossing kinematics and `runScript`, which drives a mounted program with a list of steps, key presses, pauses and snapshots) and the fixture constants of `support/m5-fixtures.ts`, whose unit test now also pins the numbers the specs derive by hand to the fixtures' sources (a fixture cannot drift from its derivation unnoticed). `examples/` is untouched.
2. **Sensor enter/exit counts on the engine** (`physics_sensor`). A sensor zone (a static box, y from 3 to 5, `sensor: true`) and four balls of radius 0.25: A dropped from y = 9 (enters through the top, leaves through the bottom), B thrown up at 12 m/s (up through the zone and out of the top, apex 7.6 m, down through it again), C thrown up at 8.5 m/s (turns inside the zone at 3.9 m and leaves downwards) and D dropped onto a static ledge inside the zone (enters, rests inside). **Derived:** 1/1, 2/2, 1/1 and 1/0 enters and exits; a ball is in contact with the zone when its centre is within the radius of it (y = 5.25 and y = 2.75), the times of the crossings follow from `y(t) = y₀ + v₀t − gt²/2`, and a crossing at time *t* after the tick that applied the start velocity is delivered in tick `offset + ceil(t / step) + 1` (the engine reports a contact one step after the poses that make it, 0063 item 5). **Measured:** the counts at the zone and at every ball are exactly those, each pair once per enter and once per exit and once to each side; the nine deliveries at the zone are in the derived order (enter before exit, then by slot, A before D in tick 54) in ticks `[20, 29, 39, 54, 54, 69, 88, 121, 139]` against the derived `[20, 29, 38, 54, 54, 69, 88, 121, 139]` (B's exit through the top is at 31.96 steps, 0.04 of a step from a tick boundary; the spec allows one tick); D rests at 4.2499, the others at 0.2499 (within 10 mm of the derived height); the ledge, which is a static body inside the zone, produced no event (two static bodies never make a pair, `strangers` = 0).
3. **The high-speed limitation, measured** (`physics_tunnel`, `physics-tunnelling.spec.ts`, `tunnelling.test.ts`). The adapter enables no continuous collision detection and changes none of Rapier's parameters (0063 item 3). The specification asks whether a fast thin-wall crossing tunnels. **The answer has two halves.**
   - **Model (derived, then confirmed).** The engine looks for contacts once per fixed step on the poses the previous step left, so a ball flying along +x is seen by a wall of thickness *t* (midplane at *c*) at the samples `x_k = x₀ + k·v·dt`. A contact exists when the surface distance is below the prediction distance *p* = 20 mm (0063 item 5); if the ball's centre is still before the midplane the contact stops it, if it is past it the contact pushes it on. The ball is stopped exactly when a sample lies in the window `[c − t/2 − r − p, c)`, of length `w = t/2 + r + p`: **0.12 m for the fixture (t = 0.1 m, r = 0.05 m)**. Consequences: a displacement per step `v·dt ≤ w` can never skip the window, so **no phase of the start tunnels at or below `w/dt` = 7.2 m/s** (at 1/60 s); above it the outcome depends on the phase of the samples against the window, and the share of start phases that are stopped is `w / (v·dt)` (0.8 at 8 m/s, 0.6 at 12 m/s, 0.12 at 60 m/s); a ball that is not stopped flies on at its own speed.
   - **Against a dynamic plate the model holds, lane by lane** (a 10 000 kg plate per lane, 17 lanes from 3 to 240 m/s, 0.05 to 4 m per step, each lane's start chosen so that the decisive samples are at least 12 mm from the window's edges): the lanes with `v ≤ 7.2` m/s (3, 6, 6, 7, 7 m/s) are all stopped, and of the lanes above it the ones whose samples miss the window pass (9 m/s from x = 3.065 ends at x = 21.065, 12 m/s from 3.24 at 27.240, 30 m/s from 3.84 at 63.840, 60 m/s from 3.84 at 123.840, 120 m/s from 2.84 at 242.840, 240 m/s from 0.84 at 480.840: exactly `x₀ + v·ticks·dt`) and the ones that catch it are stopped at x = 4.900 to 4.916. The adapter's own sweep (`tunnelling.test.ts`, 40 phases at each of ten speeds) agrees in every phase whose decisive sample is not within 4 mm of an edge, and the share of stopped phases is the derived `0.12 m / step` within 0.08; a scratch scan of the same 40 phases (not asserted to the digit) stopped 36/40 at 8 m/s, 32/40 at 9, 24/40 at 12, 19/40 at 15, 9/40 at 30 and 4/40 at 60, against the derived 0.90, 0.80, 0.60, 0.48, 0.24 and 0.12 (the samples are discrete, so the shares are quantised).
   - **Against a static wall nothing passes: measured, not derived.** In the browser every ball of all 17 lanes is stopped at the face (x = 4.900, up to 240 m/s); in Node the sweep covers speeds from 3 to 100 000 m/s (1 667 m per step), walls of 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001 m and balls of 0.05 and 0.005 m from two starts: always stopped at the face (within 10 mm). Scratch runs (not asserted) agree: a ball that hits at an angle is stopped, a ball that hits the edge of the wall is stopped, a ball that clears the edge flies on, as it should, and a ball against a wall turned 45 degrees is deflected along it. So **the engine protects the pair of a dynamic body and a fixed one at any speed this task could produce; the limitation is the pair of two moving bodies** (two dynamic bodies, a dynamic body and a kinematic one). **Hypothesis:** the engine predicts contacts against fixed colliders along the whole motion of the step; no test pins the mechanism, only the outcome. **Measured, not asserted:** enabling `setCcdEnabled(true)` on every dynamic body (a scratch mutation of the adapter) changed nothing in the dynamic-plate lanes in this engine, so CCD is not a v0.1 mitigation to point authors at; a kinematic wall swept onto a resting ball follows the same window from 6 m/s up and deviates at 3 m/s in some phases (the wall's centre must pass the ball's before the ball is carried), which this record does not characterise further.
   - **Guidance recorded in `spec/physics.md` §7:** keep `v·dt` at or below `t/2 + r + 0.02` m for thin obstacles that move or are hit by moving bodies, make thin obstacles static where the program allows, and expect that a ball of radius 0.05 m against a 0.1 m wall begins to tunnel above 7.2 m/s at the 1/60 s step. The numbers scale: the window is `t/2 + r + 0.02` and the threshold speed is that over the fixed step.
4. **Removal and creation inside collision callbacks, on the engine** (`physics_callbacks`). No gravity; every ball flies at 5 m/s (4 m/s for the twins) into a static wall or into another ball. **Derived (counted from the program, delivery tick `2 + ceil((gap − 0.02) / (closing speed · dt))`):** three Mines hit the Pad in one tick (34): the Pad counts three enters and no exit (the Mines are gone before they could leave, and the events of removed bodies are dropped, 0063), each Mine drops a `Mark{1}` and destroys itself without disturbing the other pairs of the iteration; the Victim hits the Killer, whose handler (lower slot) destroys `other`, so the Victim's own handler never runs (no `Mark{2}` ever exists); two Twins meet head on (tick 44) and the lower slot's handler spawns a `Mark{3}` and destroys `other` and `self`, so the second delivery of the pair is skipped (exactly one `Mark{3}`, and no `W8032`, which a second destroy of a pending entity would report); a Breeder hits the Nursery and spawns a `Mark{4}` and three Sparks (dynamic bodies) and destroys itself. **Measured:** exactly these, in ticks 34 and 44 (derived 34 and 44); bodies 10, then 8 (3 Mines, the Victim and the Breeder removed, 3 Sparks created: after the frame of the callbacks, at its flush, never inside the callbacks), then 6 (the three static bodies and the Sparks); `entitiesSpawned` 15, `entitiesDestroyed` 7, nothing pending at the end of any of the 120 frames, no diagnostic, no frame error, and `physicsColliders` equal to `physicsBodies` in every frame.
5. **Pause and resume without a jump** (`physics-pause.spec.ts`, on `physics_drop`). Manual clock: run A steps 15 + 25 ticks, run B steps 15, pauses, steps 205 more frames (the manual clock does not advance a paused application) and presses Space for five of them (discarded: input during a pause is dropped), resumes and steps 25. **Measured:** during the pause the step counter stays at 15, the pose and the scene state do not change, `discardedSteps` stays 0; after the resume B ends bit for bit where A ends (the pose is the free-fall pose of 40 ticks, 0.807 m, `rise` is the velocity of tick 39, −6.3765 m/s: no impulse), 40 steps in both. Real clock (`requestAnimationFrame`, `test: { renderTarget }`): paused 700 ms of wall-clock time (measured ≥ 650 ms) the counter and the pose are identical before and after; after the resume every read has the free-fall pose of its step count (`y₀ − g·dt²(n²/2 + n/8)`, within 1 mm) and the steps since the resume never exceed `floor(elapsed / dt) + 1`. Mutating the scheduler so that `resume()` keeps the last timestamp makes this spec fail with 4 steps against at most 1 (the four catch-up steps of the clamped 0.1 s frame).
6. **Same-frame combinations** (`physics_combos`, `physics-combinations.spec.ts`). A control run measures the tick K in which a ball launched at 5 m/s reaches the Pad (measured 34, derived 34); the combinations place their input around it. **Measured, and the documented outcome of each:** spawn then destroy in one handler creates nothing (`entitiesSpawned` and `entitiesDestroyed` unchanged, no body, no buffer allocated, no diagnostic); the ball destroyed by a key handler in the frame of tick K (removed at the flush before the step), by the scene's `fixed_update` in tick K (pending when the step produces the contact) delivers no event and runs none of its own handlers, and destroyed in tick K + 1 (by either route) the event of tick K was delivered first (`pad_hits` 1, `pad_tick` 34, one Mark); in every case the ball is gone, nothing pending, three bodies left; key down and key up of one key within one frame run both handlers in order (log `[1, 2]`) and the later `set_linear_velocity` of the lane wins (velocity (0, 0, 3): after one tick z = −20 + 3·dt, x = 0; after 30 ticks z = −18.5); input during a pause is discarded (W released and E pressed while paused: no `key_up`/`key_down` until the resume, then the synthesised release of W only, `is_key_down` polling reads false, no E); and one frame of three ticks (3.5 steps, so exactly three whatever the rounding of the step) with a key that destroys a body, spawns a body, spawns and destroys another and, in the middle tick, the ball reaching the Pad: three steps ran, the destroyed body's body left at the first flush, the spawned one was created there and moved twice (`2 · 6 · dt` = 0.2000 along +x: its velocity is queued by its first `fixed_update` and applied one tick later, decision 0066 item 4), the Pad was delivered once in tick K and its handler's Mark exists (spawned in tick K, created at the next flush), the cancelled spawn never existed, slots: the spawned body took a **new** slot (the destroyed body's was still occupied when it reserved), the Mark took one of the two slots that flush freed under generation 1, and the next frame's spawn reuses slots under generation 1 (`spec/scenes.md` §9; decision 0064 item 10).
7. **Twenty mount/dispose cycles with live crates** (`physics-resources.spec.ts`, on `physics_waves`; the test of decision 0066 covered bodies and colliders only). Each cycle mounts, throws a wave of 50 crates (40 ticks, so they are alive and in contact) and disposes. **Measured:** before dispose `physicsBodies` 51, `liveBuffers` 12, `liveBindGroups` 6, `liveTextures` 2, `livePipelines` 2, 3 shared parameter blocks, identical in all 20 cycles (`uploads`, `uploadBytes`, `buffersAllocated` 21, `bindGroupsCreated` 6 too: nothing accumulates between cycles); after `dispose()` bodies, colliders, buffers, textures, samplers, shader modules, pipelines, bind groups and the runtime's `liveListeners` are all 0, and a spy on `EventTarget.addEventListener`/`removeEventListener` shows that all 9 listeners a mount adds are removed again (`spec/testing.md` §6.6). **Observation, not changed:** after `dispose()` the *entity* counters still read what the scene had (`liveEntities` 51, `entitySlots` 51): they describe the CPU scene object the caller may still hold, not a resource, and `spec/runtime-abi.md` §9.3 promises zero live *objects* of the GPU and the physics world; the spec asserts the resources only. If the owner wants the entity counters at zero after dispose too, it is a one-line change in `counters()` and a test.
8. **1 000 spawns and destroys in waves: a bounded plateau** (`physics_waves`). Waves of 50 crates (ten columns of five, three tints) that destroy themselves after 90 ticks over a static floor; twenty of them are 1 000 crates, then three of 100 (key D) and three more of 50, 1 450 in all; counters read with the crates alive (30 frames in) and after they were all destroyed (100 frames in). **Derived:** the physics world follows the wave exactly (`physicsBodies` = `physicsColliders` = `liveEntities` = 1 + wave size at the peak, 1 after), `entitySlots` is 1 + the largest wave so far (51, then 101: the table never shrinks, LIFO reuse keeps it bounded, decision 0064 item 8), nothing is pending, 3 shared parameter blocks, 3 instanced draws and 4 draw calls at the peak, 1 draw call after. **Measured plateau:**

   | | live buffers | live bind groups | textures | pipelines | owned blocks | entity slots | `buffersAllocated` | `bindGroupsCreated` |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | mounted, no wave | 11 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 11 | 3 |
   | wave 1 (50), peak | 12 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 51 | 21 | 8 |
   | wave 1, end | 12 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 51 | 21 | 8 |
   | wave 20 (1 000 crates), peak and end | 12 | 6 / 3 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 51 | 21 | 65 |
   | wave 21 (first of 100), peak | 12 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 101 | 23 | 69 |
   | wave 26 (1 450 crates), end | 12 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 101 | 23 | 84 |

   Every live count at the peak and at the end of waves 2 to 20 equals wave 1's (asserted), the cumulative counters (`buffersAllocated`, `texturesAllocated`, `pipelinesCreated`, `shaderModulesCreated`) do not move after wave 1, a wave of 100 moves the plateau **once** (two buffers allocated, slots 101) and waves 22 to 26 are on it. **The caches, explained.** (a) The slot table keeps its capacity (51, then 101). (b) The uniform arenas double their capacity when a slot is needed and the instance buffer grows to `max(records, 2 × old)`; neither shrinks (decision 0064 items 13 and 14, `gpu/uniform-arena.ts`, `render/renderer.ts` `ensureInstances`). The ten buffers the first wave allocates are those growths (the object arena goes 1 → 64 slots, six doublings, and the instance buffer and the arenas of the shared blocks grow with the visible crates); the **two** of the first wave of 100 are, by the same code, one more doubling of the object arena (64 → 128) and one growth of the instance buffer (50 → 100). That attribution is read from the code after the measurement and is consistent with the counters (the `+2` of the 100-wave is exactly those two growths); the counters do not say which buffer is which, so it is a **hypothesis** that no test pins. The one extra *live* buffer (11 → 12) is the instance buffer, the only buffer a scene without spawned entities does not have (same status). The pipelines (2), textures (2: the depth texture and the offscreen target) and the physics world are not caches that grow: they are made once. (c) **Not a leak, but churn worth knowing:** `bindGroupsCreated` grows by exactly three per wave once the plateau is reached (8 after wave 1, 65 = 8 + 19 · 3 after wave 20, 84 after wave 26; the first wave of 100 adds four, one more than the three, which is the bind group of the grown arena, a hypothesis): the three are the bind groups of the three shared parameter blocks, created when a block gets its first member and released with its last. Live bind groups are 6 at a peak and 3 after, constant; a program that spawns and destroys all the members of a block every frame would create one per block per frame. A cache of empty shared blocks would remove the churn; it is not needed for a bounded steady state and is left to the optimisation work.
9. **Mutation checks** (each was applied by hand, the specs run, and the change reverted): `resume()` that keeps the last timestamp fails the real-clock pause spec (4 steps against 1); delivering the second entity of a pair without the aliveness check fails the callbacks spec (a second `Mark{3}`, `W8032`) and the same-frame destroy-in-tick case (the ball's handler ran); `entityRemoved` that does not remove the body fails the waves spec at wave 1 (bodies never return to 1); a prediction distance of 40 mm in the adapter (`normalizedPredictionDistance`) fails the tunnelling spec (lane 06: 9 m/s from 3.065 is stopped instead of passing) and six speeds of the Node sweep; `setCcdEnabled(true)` on dynamic bodies did **not** fail it, which is the measured fact of item 3.
10. **Not in this record (honest scope).** Kinematic movers are not characterised (item 3). The tunnelling numbers are for a sphere against a box at the 1/60 s step; other shapes (a box ball tumbled and bounced in a scratch run) and other steps follow the same window in principle (`t/2 + r + p` over the step) but were not measured. The mechanism by which fixed colliders are protected is not established (hypothesis). The per-buffer attribution of the allocations is not measured (item 8). The entity counters after dispose are an observation (item 7). The real-clock pause spec depends on `requestAnimationFrame` running at a normal rate in the headless browser; its bounds are in elapsed wall-clock time so a slower rate cannot make it fail, only a simulated jump can. Interpolation, device recovery with physics and hot reload of velocities are decisions 0066's.

## Consequences

- `spec/physics.md` §7 is rewritten to point at the evidence and to state the measured limitation (the window, 7.2 m/s, static walls); `spec/testing.md` §1 lists the specs; `spec/scenes.md` §10.3 and `spec/runtime-abi.md` §9.3 name the specs that carry their conformance tests.
- A program author gets one number for thin moving obstacles: `v·dt ≤ t/2 + r + 0.02`; thin fixed walls are safe.
- The resource plateau of a physics program is the largest wave's arenas, instance buffer and entity table; nothing grows with the number of waves. The per-wave bind-group churn (3 per wave for 3 shared blocks) is recorded, not changed.
