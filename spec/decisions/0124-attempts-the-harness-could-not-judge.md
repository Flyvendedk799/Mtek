# 0124. Attempts the model never answered are not judged: a call without a response is retried, ends `error` / `transport-error`, is not charged when nothing was sent, and the report reads the old records the same way; the M7.1 comparison

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-19
- Blueprint origin: M7 (blueprint §11), task M7-05 and its re-run M7.1; `spec/ai-and-benchmarks.md` §§1, 4 and 6.4.
- Supersedes: nothing. Amends decision 0077 item 13 and decision 0108 items 5(d) and 6 (a failed generation is "never retried"; a call that returned no usable answer is charged its worst case) for the one case below, and applies decision 0108 item 10 (an attempt the harness could not judge is reported apart and stays out of the completion rate) to it.

## Context

The M7.1 re-run of the Mtek conditions on the main set (`benchmarks/results/ai/m7-1-rerun-main/`, 34 tasks x 5 trials x two conditions = 340 attempts, Claude Haiku 4.5, temperature 1) lost 57 attempts to the transport. Every one of them has exactly one model call with generation `backend-error`, finish `error`, no response text, no tokens and the error text `Connection error.` (52) or `Request timed out.` (5); the attempt then ended `failure` / `generation-error`. The 57 calls started between 2026-10-19T17:18:27Z and 17:19:28Z, directly after the last call that was answered (17:18:18Z), and no call in that window was answered. The 113 failed calls of the two re-runs that say `Connection error.` returned in 11 to 498 ms (all but one within 14 ms: nothing was waited for); the 7 that say `Request timed out.` (5 in the main re-run, 2 in the holdout re-run) took 10.1 to 10.7 s, which looks like a 10 s connect timeout (an inference: the SDK drops the cause of a timeout, so the record does not say). The holdout re-run started at 17:19:32Z and lost all 80 attempts: 63 to the same two errors (17:19:32Z to 17:20:04Z), 17 refused by the spend cap (generation `budget-exceeded`, budget `spend`), because the guard had charged each failed call its worst case. In all 120 failed calls were charged 3.96 USD of the 21.98 USD on the ledger (cap 22.00); what the service really billed is unknown and at most that.

The report counted the 57 as failures of the model, which they are not: the model was never reached. Decision 0108 item 10 already says that an attempt the harness could not judge is reported apart and stays out of the completion rate and of tokens per completed task, but it names only not-run, error, unavailable and unfinished-by-budget, and the harness had no way to say "the network was down" other than `failure`. The effect on the numbers is large: 57 of 340 attempts (16.8 %) are in the denominator as failures; they are all of trial index 4, the last trial of a trial-major run (of its 68 attempts the first 11 were answered and the last 57 lost); and the owner stopped spending, so they will not be re-run.

## Decision

**The harness (for future runs)**

1. **A call that gets no response is retried.** A model call that fails before any response (a connection error, a timeout, DNS) is retried with a growing pause: at most three retries, after 2 s, 8 s and 30 s (`TRANSPORT_RETRY_DELAYS_MS`). The retry is the same request (same prompt hash). Each try is a `model-call` record of its own with its own error, usage and charge; the first try of a round has no `retry` key (or 0), the n-th retry has `retry: n`. The retries count in the attempt's `calls`. The wall-clock budget of the attempt runs through the pauses and no retry is made once it is spent; an interrupt (Ctrl-C) ends a pause at once and no further retry is made. A response with an HTTP status (4xx, 5xx, overloaded), a refusal, a truncation, a cancellation, an unexpected stop reason and a client that throws are NOT retried and keep their handling (a failure, `generation-*`).
2. **What counts as "no response" is decided by the client.** `GenerateResult.transport` is `connection` (no HTTP exchange completed and the request most likely never reached the service: refused, DNS, no route, a connect timeout, or a connection failure with no known code) or `timeout` (the request was or may have been sent and no answer came: a reset, a socket error, a header or body timeout, the SDK's own timeout). The `anthropic` adapter derives it from the SDK's error class and from the cause of the fetch that failed (it remembers that cause per call, because the SDK words a connect timeout as `Request timed out.` and drops its cause); the chat-completions clients (`openai-compatible`, `llama-cpp`) mark a rejected fetch (`timeout` for a `TimeoutError`/`AbortError`, else `connection`) and now also return the `httpStatus` of an error response; the replay client reads `errorKind` and `httpStatus` back from a recorded run. The SDK's own retries stay off (`maxRetries: 0`): the loop retries, so that every try is recorded and charged.
3. **If there is still no response the attempt is not judged.** It ends outcome `error`, reason `transport-error` (message: the error, "no response from the model", the number of retries), never `failure`. If a repair round fails this way the first attempt (the validation of the first reply) stays recorded. The record schema (`spec/ai-eval-record.schema.json`, version 1, keys and one enum value added, old records stay valid) gains `model-call.errorKind` (`connection`, `timeout`, `http` or null), `model-call.httpStatus`, `model-call.retry`, the trial-end reason `transport-error`, and a rule that this reason goes with the outcome `error`. The keys are written only when they apply, so a record of an answered call is byte for byte what it was. `benchmark-run` ends such a run as it ends any other (status `complete`, exit 0) and prints a warning line with the number of attempts that got no response.
4. **The spend guard.** A call that failed with `transport: connection` and returned no response is charged 0 and noted `no response: not charged` in the ledger entry (nothing was sent, or nothing came back, so the service cannot have billed it; this is an assumption about the service, stated here). A `timeout` (the request was or may have been sent) is still charged its worst case, as is a call that threw and a call whose usage was not reported; an HTTP error response is charged nothing, as before. Each retry is reserved and charged on its own, so a retried call can neither hide nor double a charge. The ledger keeps what it already holds: its 120 worst-case charges for the M7.1 failures (3.96 USD) are not corrected (the file is append-only and its total is checked against its entries).

**The report (for the records that exist)**

5. **A legacy rule.** `src/report.ts` reads an attempt as `error` / `transport-error` (not judged) when its record says `failure` / `generation-error` AND every model call of it is `backend-error` with the error text `Connection error.` or `Request timed out.`, no response text, no tokens (0 in and 0 out) and no HTTP status. An attempt with any other call (even one answered call), with any other error text or with a status stays a failure. The records are not changed. Such an attempt has no first attempt (it is out of the first-attempt statistics). The report prints, under "Attempts that could not be judged", how many attempts ended `transport-error`, by condition, that they are out of every completion rate and of tokens per completed task, and, when any were read by this rule, the rule itself in these words; a report with no such attempt prints nothing new, so the reports already stored are unchanged. The holdout summary carries the same outcome and reason.
6. **`report-cli --baseline-run DIR`** adds only the baseline attempts of an earlier run (`selectSide`), so a re-run of Mtek can be judged against a baseline that was not re-run by the rule of decision 0108 item 11 without the earlier run's Mtek cells joining the re-run's (the context-only condition has the same condition id in both runs, because the id records the kind of documentation, not the text the compiler produced).
7. **`compare-cli`** (`src/compare.ts`) writes a before/after comparison of the Mtek conditions of stored runs, from the records only: per condition, for all attempts of each run as published and for the matched cells (the cells, a task with a trial index, that are judged in both runs, so both sides cover the same cells), the attempts, completed tasks, completion rate and tokens per completed task, overall, per mode and per category; the baseline row copied from the runs before; what was lost, by condition, with the trial index of the lost attempts. It refuses a side that repeats a condition and leaves holdout attempts out. It is not a verdict: the verdict against the baseline is the report's.

**The M7.1 documents**

8. `benchmarks/results/ai/report-m7-1.md` (the re-run's Mtek attempts, the baseline attempts of `m7-05-main`; a test regenerates it byte for byte), `benchmarks/results/ai/compare-m7-1.md` (likewise) and `benchmarks/results/ai/holdout-summary-m7-1.jsonl` (ids, outcomes, reasons and layer outcomes of the holdout re-run: 63 `error` / `transport-error` and 17 `unfinished` / `budget-spend`, none judged). The holdout run directory is outside the repository, so that file is not regenerated by a test; its form and counts are checked. The evidence page is `evidence/M7/ai-benchmark-m7-1.md`. **No holdout result exists for the new documentation.** No retry of the lost attempts was run; the harness change above has not met the service (the tests use fake transports).

## Consequences

- Attempts lost to a dead network no longer look like model failures in any new run, and cost nothing when nothing was sent. A real outage still loses attempts (three retries are 40 s), but they are `error`, listed, and out of the rate.
- A run that meets an outage will still run through its remaining attempts, each trying four times with 40 s of pauses; stopping a run after a number of consecutive transport errors is not built (the owner or the cap does that).
- The attempts that the legacy rule reads (57 in the main re-run, 63 in the holdout re-run) are an interpretation of old records by a stated rule, not a change of them; anyone can recompute the old reading from the same file (a failure with a `generation-error`).
- The M7.1 figures are not a re-measurement of the same cells: the lost cells are 28 and 29 cells of trial index 4, so the comparison with the earlier runs is on the matched cells and says so.
