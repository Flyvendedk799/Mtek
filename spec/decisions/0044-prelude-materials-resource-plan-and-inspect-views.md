# 0044. Prelude materials, the resource plan and the inspect views: details the specification leaves open

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-04
- Blueprint origin: §4.4 (GPU code is only what stages reach), §6.3 (update classes and sharing), §10 (inspectability); `spec/materials.md` §4, §9; `spec/gpu-layout.md` §5, §6, §8.2, §8.3, §10; `spec/tooling.md` §1; `spec/compiler-architecture.md` §4.8–§4.12; decisions 0013, 0024, 0028, 0029, 0030, 0032, 0036, 0039, 0041.

## Context

Task M2-09 removes the temporary M1 `Unlit` of decision 0013 (`lowering/builtin_unlit.rs`, a shader IR built by the compiler): the built-in materials are compiled from the embedded prelude `std/materials.mtek` like user materials. It also completes the resource plan (instances, update classes, sharing, a layout per material, the `E6001` limit) and adds `mtek inspect --shaders` and `mtek inspect --bindings`. The specification says that the prelude is "compiled by the same pipeline", what the plan decides and what `--bindings` shows, but not where the prelude joins the pipeline, how its names relate to the registry's, what happens to `Pbr` before M4, who reports `E6001`, or the shapes of the two views. They are fixed here so that they are visible, testable and changeable by a later record.

## Decision

All of the following are **proposals** (design choices), not external constraints.

1. **The prelude is a module of the program.** When an entity of a scene of any module has a material that is a descriptor of a registry material schema (`Unlit`), the front end (`src/prelude.rs`, called from `check.rs`) adds `std/materials.mtek` — the embedded text of `stdlib/std/materials.mtek`, the registry's `prelude_sources` — to the source map after the project's sources and to the module graph after the project's modules, then lexes, parses, resolves and type-checks it and includes it in the effect analysis (its fragment stage is a GPU root). `check`, `inspect` and `build` therefore see it alike; the typed IR holds it as its **last module**, so `mtek inspect --ir` shows the built-in materials (spec `materials.md` §9: "inspectable"). A program without built-in materials does not load it, so its sources, manifest and build id are unchanged (decision 0030 item 7). `std/` stays reserved (`E9001`, `E2031`, decisions 0030 and 0036); built-in materials need no import.
2. **Prelude mode of the resolver** (`resolve::resolve_prelude_module`): a `material` declaration of the prelude may take the name of a registry material schema, which it defines; every other prelude-name rule is unchanged (`E2001` for anything else, also in the prelude). A user module declaring `material Unlit` stays `E2001`.
3. **Milestone gating.** Before resolution, a prelude `material` whose registry schema's milestone this build has not reached is left out of the parsed module (`Pbr`, M4); the text and its spans stay as embedded. Using `Pbr` stays the resolver's `E9010` in the user's module.
4. **Defects.** The prelude is compiled into a sink of its own; any diagnostic it produces is reported as one `E9999` ("The embedded prelude 'std/materials.mtek' could not be compiled; this is a compiler bug.") naming each diagnostic. Tests compile it without diagnostics.
5. **Instances.** A built-in instance in the IR names the prelude's material item (`std/materials.mtek::Unlit`, decision 0028 item 3), its param types taken from that item; the IR lowering no longer reads the registry for it. The prelude's `Unlit` agrees with the registry's `Unlit` schema (names, types, defaults) by test.
6. **Spans.** A built-in material's declaration span is its IR item's span, `material Unlit { … }` without `export`, as for user materials; its shader's span map points at the stage, the statements and the expressions they come from (the temporary path mapped everything to the whole declaration). The WGSL is unchanged (`tests/codegen/wgsl/unlit.wgsl`, the same hash).
7. **Resource plan** (`plan::plan_program`). From the IR of the entry scene: meshes as before; one instance per entity with a material, in entity order, each param with the update class the IR recorded (`initial` in M2) and the instance span; `shareable` when every param is `initial`; the **materials** the instances use, sorted by symbol, each with its name, declaration, params (types as written, decision 0041 item 1) and parameter block, all taken from the material item (user and built-in alike). An instance of a material that is not an item of the program, or whose params differ from the item's, is a defect.
8. **`E6001`** (`plan::check_limits`) is checked by `build` and by `inspect --shaders`/`--bindings` after planning and before any shader is lowered: for every planned material whose block is larger than `maxUniformBufferBindingSize` of the target profile (65 536 for `webgpu-core-2026`, `TargetProfile::max_uniform_buffer_binding_size`), at the material declaration, with the largest param as a related span, a note naming the limit and a help. It is an emit-phase limit: `check` does not report it (`spec/tooling.md` §1, "parse + type-check"), so its coverage is `tests/build.rs`, not a semantic fixture. Only materials the entry scene uses are checked, because only they are built.
9. **Manifest.** `materials`, `materialInstances`, `layouts` (material blocks) and the material and param `symbols` are written from the plan; nothing in them changed for existing programs.
10. **`--shaders`.** For every planned material, in plan order: `material`, `declaration` (source location), `hash`, `url`, `map`, the entry points, `vertexAttributes`, `surfaceInputs`, `layout` (id or `null`), the `wgsl` text — exactly the bytes `build` writes — and the whole span map with source locations (`{ wgsl: { line, colStart, colEnd }, source, symbol }`). A source location is the `source` object of the JSON diagnostics. The human form prints the interface and the WGSL with line numbers; a line is followed by `// <file:line:col-line:col> <symbol>` of its widest entry whenever that differs from the previous annotated line (the span-map summary).
11. **`--bindings`** (`spec/gpu-layout.md` §10). `blocks`: `builtin:frame`, `builtin:object`, then the material blocks by id (the order of the manifest's `layouts`), each the layout record of §5 flattened into the object, then `group`, `binding`, `dynamicOffset`, `visibility`, `owner` (`runtime`, `object`, `material instance`), `material` for a material block, and `fields`: struct members flattened recursively (`wave.amplitude`), an array as one field with `length`, `stride`, `padded`, each with `mtekType`, `wgslType` (the declaration's WGSL type), `offset`, `size`, `padding` (bytes to the next field or the end of its struct) and, for a material block's top-level fields, the `param` declaration. `instances`: `index`, `material`, `entity`, `entityIndex`, `layout`, `group`, `binding` (`null` without a block), `shareable` and per param `name`, `type`, `declaration`, `instance`, `update`, `dependencies` (empty before `bind`, M3), `field`, `offset`, `size`. The human form is one table per block and per instance. Both views start with `scene` and `targetProfile`.
12. **Library and CLI.** `Inspect` gains `Shaders` and `Bindings`; `InspectResult` keeps `ir` and gains `what`, `shaders` and `bindings`; `render` dispatches on `what` and gives `None` with errors. `package::checked_plan` and `package::material_shaders` are the steps `build` and `inspect` share. The CLI maps the three exclusive flags to the views; output rules are those of `--ir` (decision 0032): the view on stdout, warnings on stderr, the report and exit 1 with errors. This amends decision 0032 item 1, which made `--bindings`/`--shaders` usage errors until M2.

## Consequences

- No new dependency and no new diagnostic code; `E6001` leaves the not-yet-implemented list.
- The IR goldens of programs with built-in materials gain the module `std/materials.mtek`; the manifests' `Unlit` declaration span starts at `material`, and their span maps reference three more spans. Goldens of programs without built-in materials are unchanged.
- **M2-10** (the M2 gate) can assert that no file or symbol of the temporary path remains (`tests/temporary_unlit_is_gone.rs`) and use `inspect --bindings`/`--shaders` as evidence; every material of a program builds through one path.
- **M2-12** and **M3**: `ParamClass` gains `imperative` and `bound` (with the binding's dependencies in `--bindings`) when the IR records them; `shareable` then follows. `E6002` arrives with texture params (M4), and `Pbr` compiles by itself when its registry milestone is reached.
- `spec/compiler-architecture.md` §4.9, §4.10 and §4.12, `spec/gpu-layout.md` §10 and `spec/materials.md` §9 point here.
