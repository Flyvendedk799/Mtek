# 0089. The benchmark task scene-rendering-11: textured surfaces on both sides, the asset a task provides, and the asynchronous, image-loading baseline support

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Blueprint origin: M6 (blueprint §11), the scene/rendering part of task M6-08 and its known gap; blueprint §12.2; `spec/ai-and-benchmarks.md` §6 (task format, baseline fairness); decisions 0021 (task format, one definition of success for both sides, the baseline hooks), 0078 (the scene tasks; item 6 left textures out), 0086 (the baseline frame order and hooks), and for the Mtek side `spec/assets.md`, `spec/materials.md` §5, decisions 0054, 0058, 0060, 0082.
- Supersedes: nothing. Closes the gap "no texture/sampler task (the baseline support has no image loader)" of `benchmarks/README.md` and of decisions 0078 item 6, 0083 and 0086.

## Context

The 30 tasks and 8 holdout tasks have no textured surface. Decision 0078 dropped the idea because the common harness could not give both sides the same image: `startTask` built the application synchronously and `test/build.ts` bundled only `src/main.ts`, so a three.js solution had no way to load a PNG before the first frame. A textured task is a distinct skill for both stacks (an `asset.texture` declaration, a material that samples it and a sampler preset on the Mtek side; an image loader, a colour space, filters and wrapping on the three.js side) and it is where a wrong colour space, a flipped `v` or a wrong filter shows in pixels. Every choice below is a **proposal** unless a source is named.

## Decision

1. **The task.** `benchmarks/tasks/scene-rendering-11` (category `scene-rendering`, mode `cold`, budgets 3 repairs, 4000 output tokens, 300 s like `-01` to `-10`). The prompt describes a scene `Demo`: clear colour `#1b2430`, a perspective camera at (0, 11, 3) looking at the origin (field of view 0.72, near 0.1, far 1000) and **three flat unlit squares** of 3.6 x 3.6 units in the plane y = 0, facing up, showing the project's image `assets/swatch.png` (4 x 4 pixels, sixteen different colours, sRGB): `Plain` at (-2, 0, -2) shows it once with hard edges; `Tiled` at (0, 0, 2) shows it twice across and twice down, hard-edged; `Smooth` at (2, 0, -2) shows it once, magnified with bilinear interpolation between pixel centres and clamped at the outer pixel centres. The picture is upright and unmirrored seen from above with +Z at the bottom (its top edge along the smaller-z edge, its left edge along the smaller-x edge). The prompt states the observable colour rule instead of an API: a hard-edged pixel `#bc4020` appears on the screen as `#bc4020`, and smoothing blends in linear light. `required_symbols`: `Demo.Plain`, `Demo.Tiled`, `Demo.Smooth`. The tolerance is stated: 2 per channel, 3 on `Smooth`.
2. **What it tests**: loading an image as an asset (a project file the compiler or the loader must find), the sRGB handling of a colour texture (a hard-edged texel must come out unchanged), the orientation conventions of the picture on a plane (which edge is the top), nearest versus linear filtering, filtering in linear light, repeat versus clamp wrapping, tiling by scaling the picture coordinates, and perspective-correct mapping on an oblique floor. It does not test lit textures (`Pbr`'s `base_color_texture`), mip-maps (Mtek v0.1 has none), non-square or large images, `asset.linear_texture` (it is the mutant of the colour space) or GLB textures.
3. **The asset.** `assets/swatch.png` is a plain 8-bit RGBA PNG without colour-space chunks, written by `evidence/M6/benchmark-texture/make-swatch.mjs` (133 bytes, deterministic). It is a **task asset shared by both sides with identical bytes**: one copy in each of `mtek/`, `baseline/`, `reference/mtek/` and `reference/baseline/`, because each project is a self-contained directory (the harness copies the Mtek project; the baseline build reads the project directory). `validate-tasks.mjs` accepts the extra directories without change (it checks the six parts and the files named in the format, not the absence of others). The sameness is guarded by a unit test (`benchmarks/baselines/support/test/assets.test.ts`: it finds every `assets/` directory of every task, compares the four copies and checks the PNG: 4 x 4, 8-bit RGBA, only `IHDR`, `IDAT`, `IEND`, 16 different opaque texels).
4. **Derivation of the expected pixels (independent of both references).** `evidence/M6/benchmark-texture/derive.mjs` (f64) transcribes the scene of the prompt, casts a ray through every pixel centre, intersects it with y = 0 and finds the panel and the picture coordinate `u = (x - cx) / 3.6 + 1/2`, `v = (z - cz) / 3.6 + 1/2` (the `Plane` convention of `spec/stdlib.md` §4 reduces to this, and so does three.js's: the prompt states the observable orientation, not either library's `uv`). The colour is the CPU sampler of `tests/browser/support/texture-reference.ts` (nearest or bilinear, clamp or repeat, sRGB decoded by hand-checked formulas, the result encoded as the `rgba8unorm-srgb` target does); nothing from three.js or the Mtek runtime. The texel-centre pixels are the pixels nearest to the **forward projection of the texel centres** through the view and projection matrices of the runtime ABI (`m1-reference.ts`), and each is confirmed by the rays. A pixel of a hard-edged panel is only used when the nine points within 1.5 pixels of its centre fall in the same texel (all 96 texel cells of `Plain` and `Tiled` qualified); a `Smooth` pixel lies at least 1.5 pixels inside its panel and its value is computed at the exact coordinate of its pixel centre; border pixels lie 1.5 to 2.5 pixels inside or outside a panel. The fixture has **171 pixels**: the 16 texels of `Plain`, the 64 of `Tiled`, for `Smooth` the 16 texel centres, 12 + 12 midpoints between two texels, 9 points between four and 16 in the outer half texels (clamp versus wrap), 18 border pixels and 8 of the clear colour. The derivation is in the comment at the head of the fixture. `selfcheck.mjs` checks the model against values worked out by hand (decoding and encoding a texel is the identity; the linear-light midpoint of `#bc4020` and `#e0a010` is `#cf7c19`, the mean of the bytes would be `#ce7018`; clamp versus repeat at u = 1/16; the camera target projects to (64, 64); rays through projected points return their coordinates) and counts per model-level mutant how many fixture pixels change.
5. **The baseline support extension** (`benchmarks/baselines/support`), existing call sites unchanged:
   - `startTask(build)`: `build` may return the application **or a promise of it** (`BuildTaskApp`, exported); `startTask` awaits it before it creates the renderer, and a rejection is reported through `window.mtekTaskError` like a synchronous failure. This is the counterpart of Mtek's `mountMtek` resolving only when every asset of the startup scene is ready (`spec/assets.md` §5). (A separate `preload` step would have meant a second callback for the same thing.)
   - `loadTexture(url, { colorSpace?, filter, wrap }): Promise<Texture>` and `configureTexture(texture, options)` (`src/texture.ts`): the counterpart of `asset.texture` (`colorSpace: "srgb"`, the default) / `asset.linear_texture` (`"linear"`) together with the sampler presets `nearest_clamp`, `nearest_repeat`, `linear_clamp`, `linear_repeat` (`filter: "nearest" | "linear"`, `wrap: "clamp" | "repeat"`; both axes, magnification and minification alike, no mip-maps like Mtek v0.1). The result is an ordinary three.js `Texture` for the `map` of any material. `loadTexture` fetches the URL, decodes with `createImageBitmap(blob, { colorSpaceConversion: "none", premultiplyAlpha: "none", imageOrientation: "flipY" })` (the runtime's decoding, `spec/assets.md` §5) and sets `flipY = false`, so the picture is upright on three.js's own `PlaneGeometry`, `BoxGeometry` and `SphereGeometry`, as a `TextureLoader` texture is. It rejects with the URL in the message when the fetch fails or the bytes are not an image. Tiling is not an option (it is the shader's `uv * k` in Mtek); the three.js side does it with `texture.repeat`, plain three.js.
   - `test/build.ts` copies the project's `assets/` directory next to the built page, so `loadTexture("assets/swatch.png", ...)` finds it (a missing `assets/` changes nothing for the other tasks).
   - The cost side: the helper is about 60 lines, counted with the rest of the support module as the baseline's documentation cost (decision 0021 item 6). The Mtek side has no helper to count: the language provides `asset.texture`, `texture` and `sampler` params and `sample`; and no built-in unlit textured material, so a solution declares a 10-line material (`Unlit` has no texture param; a finding, not changed here).
6. **Fairness.** (a) The prompt names no API (no `asset.texture`, `sampler`, `TextureLoader`, `NearestFilter`, `repeat`); where the stacks differ it states what must be seen (a hard-edged pixel keeps its stored colour; blending in linear light; the top edge of the picture). (b) The Mtek side's picture coordinates have `v` down and three.js's have `v` up: the prompt fixes the orientation in terms of the world, and the two references each get it right by their own convention; the three.js helper hides the flip as `TextureLoader` does. (c) A panel with **clamped, scaled** coordinates was deliberately left out: scaling coordinates by 2 with clamping puts the picture in the top-left quarter in Mtek (`v` down) and in the bottom-left quarter with three.js's `texture.repeat` (`v` up), a difference of the libraries a prompt cannot make fair without naming one of them. Clamp is tested at the picture's edge (`Smooth`, outer half texels) and repeat with an integer tile count, where both conventions agree. (d) The baseline starter says how the image is reached (the files of `assets/` are served next to the page; the support module has a helper that loads one) and that the build function may be async, in the same way it already says what `startTask` does; the Mtek starter stays the one-line stub and the model learns `asset.texture` from `mtek context`, which is the documentation channel of each side. This is a judgement; the two channels are not equal in size (item 5). (e) Both references were written from the prompt; neither was consulted for the fixture, and **two alternative solutions** pass the same fixture: the three.js solution without the helper (a `TextureLoader`, colour space and filters by hand) and a differently written Mtek scene (two materials, tiling as `vec2(2.0, 2.0)` in the shader, samplers per entity or by default).
7. **Mutants** (`evidence/M6/benchmark-texture/run-mutants.mjs`, results in `mutants.json`): ten plausible wrong solutions, each made from a reference by one edit, **fail the fixture on both stacks** (hardware adapter): the picture tagged linear (`asset.linear_texture` / `colorSpace: "linear"`; first failing pixel off by 73), the picture flipped in `v`, mirrored in `u`, transposed (Mtek) or turned by 90 degrees (three.js), `Plain` linear, `Tiled` linear, `Smooth` nearest, `Smooth` repeating, `Tiled` clamping, no tiling. The model-level counts of `selfcheck.mjs` (e.g. 154 of 171 pixels for the colour space, 147 for flipped `v`, 62 for a wrong wrapping, 61 for no tiling) show every kind is caught by many pixels, not one. Not covered: a texture-coordinate error of less than the tolerance (a half-pixel shift) and anything a mutant of one edit cannot express.

## Consequences

- The set has 31 tasks (11 scene-rendering) and 8 holdout tasks; the holdout and its hash are unchanged. Until the orchestrator updates them, `benchmarks/README.md` says "30 tasks" and lists a texture gap.
- **Needed from the orchestrator (not edited here):** `benchmarks/README.md`: the counts (30 to 34 with the three parallel interaction tasks, scene-rendering 10 to 11), the status table row of the tasks, the "Known benchmark gaps" cell (the texture/sampler gap is closed; key-up, held-key and `fixed_update` tasks are covered by the parallel work), the task layout (an optional `assets/` directory in each of the four projects, byte-identical copies), "How the baseline tests work" (the build function may be async; `loadTexture` and `configureTexture` among the helpers; `assets/` is served next to the page) and the baseline results section. `validate-tasks.mjs` needs **no change** (it neither fixes the task count nor rejects extra files in the project directories); an optional hardening would be a check that every `assets/` file has identical copies in the four projects, which `assets.test.ts` does now. `spec/ai-and-benchmarks.md` §6.1 could mention the optional `assets/` directory.
- Decisions 0078 item 6, 0083 and 0086 state that textures were left out; they are not rewritten (records are history) and this record supersedes that statement.
- No language, compiler or runtime behaviour changed.
